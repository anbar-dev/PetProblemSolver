Litter box problem

Your cat kicks litter out of the box.

If litter is scattered around the box immediately after digging, a larger mat alone is not the first fix. Match the box shape to your cat's digging style, keep the entrance usable, and use the mat for the granules that still escape on paws.

Quick verdict

Watch the box while your cat digs. If litter is flying over the rim before the cat leaves, start with a roomy open box with higher sides and a low, easy entrance. If litter is mostly on the first steps outside, fix the exit path with a mat. Keep enough litter for normal digging, but do not overfill the box, and change only one part of the setup at a time so your cat does not reject it.

What to try first

  1. Watch where the litter lands. Litter inside the box and beside the walls suggests digging; a trail farther away suggests paw tracking; litter in front of the entrance can mean the box is too small or the exit is awkward.
  2. Check the fill level. Use a consistent depth that lets your cat bury comfortably without leaving the box overfilled. Too much loose litter gives a vigorous digger more material to throw.
  3. Clear the rim and route. Do not put the box against a wall so close that the cat cannot turn or exit normally. Leave room for a mat without making the entrance a squeeze.
  4. Scoop before adding a new product. A dirty box can trigger frantic digging or repeated attempts to find a clean spot. Scoop daily and clean the surrounding floor.
  5. Change the box gradually. Put a new open box beside the familiar one when possible, use the same litter at first, and wait for consistent use before removing the old box.

What to skip

  • Making the box too small. A cramped box can increase digging and scatter because the cat cannot turn comfortably.
  • Using a covered box as the automatic answer. A lid may contain some scatter, but it can trap odor and make entry or exit less comfortable.
  • Filling the box to the rim. More litter is not always better; an overfilled box gives a digger more loose material to throw.
  • Blocking the escape route with a mat or storage tray. The cat should be able to leave without jumping or squeezing through a narrow gap.
  • Punishing digging or accidents. Stress can make litter box problems harder to read and may reduce reliable box use.

The 10-minute scatter reset

  1. Watch one normal box visit and note whether litter leaves during digging or after the cat exits.
  2. Scoop the box and wipe the surrounding floor so old scatter does not hide the new pattern.
  3. If the litter is leaving over the rim, test a roomy open high-sided box beside the familiar box.
  4. If the litter is leaving on paws, place a flat mat on the natural exit path.
  5. Vacuum once a day for a week and change only the part of the setup that matches the observed cause.

When gear is not enough

Ask your vet promptly about sudden changes in digging, repeated box visits, straining, crying, blood, constipation, diarrhea, vomiting, or a cat that starts eliminating outside the box. Pain, urinary problems, stress, or constipation can change how a cat uses the box. Do not rely on a taller box to hide a health or behavior problem.

FAQ

How do I stop my cat from kicking litter out of the box?

First watch whether litter is leaving during digging or after the cat exits. For digging scatter, try a roomy open box with higher sides and a usable low entrance; for paw tracking, use a mat on the exit path.

Will a high-sided litter box stop litter scatter?

It can reduce litter thrown over the rim, but it is not right for every cat. Keep the entrance accessible, avoid trapping the box in a corner, and preserve a familiar low-entry option during the change.

How much litter should I put in the box?

Use enough for comfortable digging without filling the box to the rim. Follow the litter label as a starting point, then watch whether your cat can bury normally without excessive scatter.

Why does my cat suddenly dig so much in the litter box?

A dirty box, a litter change, box discomfort, constipation, urinary problems, or stress can all alter digging. Sudden changes or other symptoms deserve a veterinary check before buying a different box.
