Scratching problem

Cat scratching the couch.

If your cat keeps shredding one sofa arm or corner, the fix is not yelling, spray bottles, or hiding every chair under plastic. Give the cat a better scratching target in the exact spot they already chose, then protect the couch long enough for the new habit to stick.

Quick verdict

For a cat scratching the couch, buy a stable vertical scratching post first and place it directly beside the damaged couch corner. Add temporary couch protection only where the cat scratches, not across the whole room. If the cat scratches low, carpet, or the front edge of the sofa, add a horizontal scratcher too. Nail trimming helps reduce damage, but it will not replace a good scratching target.

What to try before buying anything

  1. Mark the exact target. Notice whether your cat scratches the vertical arm, the front corner, the rug near the sofa, or the back of the couch.
  2. Move an existing scratcher there. If you already own a post, put it within paw reach of the damaged spot for a week.
  3. Stop hiding the good target. Scratchers tucked in a spare room rarely beat a sofa that is in the main social zone.
  4. Reward the first good scratch. Treats, praise, or play right after using the scratcher can help the redirect feel worth repeating.

What to skip

  • Spray bottles. They may stop the moment, but they do not teach the cat where to scratch instead.
  • Tiny decorative posts. If the post wobbles or does not allow a full stretch, the couch may still feel better.
  • Covering the entire sofa forever. Protect the target zone while you build the replacement habit.
  • Declawing as a furniture fix. Scratching is normal cat behavior. Use redirection, protection, and nail care instead.

The 7-day couch redirect

  1. Put the new scratcher directly beside the scratched couch spot, not across the room.
  2. Cover only the damaged target area with a tested protector or deterrent sheet.
  3. Scratch the post lightly with your fingers, then reward any interest with treats or play.
  4. Move play sessions so the cat ends near the scratcher instead of launching at the sofa.
  5. After a week of consistent scratcher use, move the post only a few inches at a time if you want it elsewhere.

When gear is not enough

Sudden destructive scratching can follow stress, boredom, household change, pain, or conflict with another pet. If scratching appears alongside hiding, appetite changes, aggression, litter box changes, overgrooming, or other new symptoms, treat it as a welfare question before buying more furniture guards.

FAQ

How do I stop my cat scratching the couch?

Place a stable scratcher right beside the damaged spot, protect the couch temporarily, and reward the cat for using the new target. The scratcher has to match the angle your cat already prefers.

Do couch protector sheets work for cats?

They can help as a temporary barrier, especially on one repeated target area. They work best when paired with a better scratcher nearby.

Is tape safe to use on a sofa?

Only use adhesive deterrents after testing a hidden spot. Avoid delicate fabrics, leather, velvet, microfiber, painted wood, or any surface where adhesive damage would be unacceptable.

Will trimming nails stop couch scratching?

No. Trimming can reduce damage from sharp tips, but it does not remove the cat's need to scratch, stretch, and mark territory.
