Quick verdict

When a cat ignores a scratching post, do not start by buying a taller version of the same thing. Watch what your cat scratches now. A cat pulling at the front of a rug may want a horizontal cardboard scratcher, while a cat stretching up a door frame needs a tall, stable vertical post. Put the replacement beside the existing target, protect only the damaged area for a short time, and reward investigation without forcing your cat's paws onto the surface.

What to try first

  1. Copy the current angle. Note whether your cat scratches vertically, horizontally, diagonally, or along a corner. Choose a new surface that lets the cat make the same movement.
  2. Move the scratcher to the habit. Place it directly beside the couch, rug, doorway, or nap spot your cat already uses. A scratcher hidden in another room has little chance against a convenient piece of furniture.
  3. Make it stable. Hold the base while your cat tests it. If it wobbles, tips, or slides, anchor it or replace it; one failed attempt can teach the cat that the old target feels better.
  4. Offer a calm invitation. Drag a toy past the scratcher, sprinkle a small amount of catnip only if your cat responds to it, or reward a sniff and a paw touch. Never grab the paws and rub them on the scratcher.
  5. Give the new habit time. Keep the scratcher in the chosen spot for at least a week before moving it. Reward use immediately with a treat, play, or quiet praise.

What to skip

  • Buying five scratchers at once. Change one variable at a time so you learn whether angle, texture, stability, or location was the problem.
  • Putting the post in a quiet corner. Scratching often happens in social, visible, or transition areas where the cat already spends time.
  • Forcing the paws onto the surface. This can make the scratcher feel threatening and does not teach a voluntary replacement habit.
  • Using punishment or spray bottles. They may interrupt the moment without showing the cat what to do instead, and can make the home feel less safe.
  • Assuming every cat likes catnip. Some cats do not respond to it, so do not judge a scratcher by a catnip test alone.

A simple 7-day reset

  1. Day 1: Watch and record the target, angle, time, and texture your cat chooses.
  2. Day 2: Put one matched scratcher directly beside that target and make sure it cannot wobble.
  3. Days 3-4: Use a short play session or a treat to create interest, then reward any voluntary use.
  4. Days 5-6: Protect only the old target if needed, checking the furniture and the cat's paws for irritation.
  5. Day 7: Keep the scratcher in place if it is being used. Move it only a few inches at a time after the habit is reliable.

When a scratcher is not the real issue

A sudden change in scratching can follow stress, boredom, household changes, conflict with another pet, or discomfort. If your cat also hides, stops eating, overgrooms, becomes unusually aggressive, changes litter box habits, or seems painful, ask your vet before treating the problem as a product failure. FAQ

Why does my cat ignore the scratching post?

The post may be in the wrong location, use the wrong texture or angle, wobble, or be too small for a full stretch. Start by matching the surface to what your cat already scratches.

How do I get my cat to use a new scratching post?

Place it beside the current target, make it stable, invite interest with play or a small reward, and praise voluntary use. Do not force the paws onto it.

Should I put catnip on a scratching post?

You can test a small amount if your cat responds to catnip, but many cats do not. Location, stability, and matching the preferred scratching angle are usually more important.

How long does it take for a cat to learn a new scratcher?

Some cats use a well-matched scratcher immediately; others need several days of easy access and rewards. Keep it in the chosen spot for at least a week before moving it.
